Dear Families and Descendants of the "Hatikvah" Ma'apilim,

On these shores, where the waves of the Mediterranean break, one of the most dramatic chapters in the story of Israel's rebirth was written. To these shores came our parents and grandparents – 1,414 heroic Holocaust survivors – following an arduous maritime journey from the port of Bocca di Magra in Italy, fighting against British destroyers for the right to build a home in the land of their ancestors.

They saw the shores of the Land of Israel and sang "Hatikvah" with tears in their eyes before being brutally deported to detention camps in Cyprus. Now, as second-generation descendants holding the torch, it is our right and historical duty to ensure that the story of their bravery is permanently engraved in the landscape of our homeland.

"Every commemorative plaque is a living memorial – a bridge connecting thousands of hikers along the coast with the heroic story of our parents on the immigration pathway."

We are acting as part of a national initiative to commemorate the legacy of clandestine immigration along the nation's coastlines. Our community's goal is clear: to establish an interactive memorial station and plaques in Haifa Bay, facing the historic arrival path of "Hatikvah".

Historical Profile: The Clandestine Immigration Vessel "Hatikvah"

Port of Departure Bocca di Magra, Italy
Date of Arrival May 17, 1947
Number of Passengers 1,414 Holocaust survivors
Fate of the Ship Intercepted by British forces after resistance; passengers deported to Cyprus detention camps
Memorial Station Location Haifa Bay
