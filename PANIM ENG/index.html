Dear Members of the "Pan" Family,

On these shores, where the waves of the Mediterranean break, one of the most dramatic and inspiring chapters in the story of Israel’s establishment unfolded. Towards these shores, some 15,500 Ma'apilim – our parents, our grandparents, and perhaps some of you – turned their eyes as they departed from Burgas, Bulgaria, in the largest operation in the history of Aliyah Bet. This operation was a resounding response to the deportation of the Exodus passengers and a testament to their unbroken spirit.

They endured an arduous journey on trains across Europe and in the crowded holds of ships, dreaming of the shores of their homeland, and sang "Hatikvah" through their tears before their painful deportation to the detention camps in Cyprus.

"Every commemorative sign is a living memorial – a bridge connecting those who walk the trail with the women, men, and crews who were aboard the ships."

The Organization of Cyprus Ma’apilim is working to install 30 commemorative signs and interactive information points along Israel’s coast, telling the stories of all 142 Aliyah Bet ships. A major initiative marking the 80th anniversaries of the Cyprus detention camps and the establishment of the State of Israel, the project aims to honor the Ma'apilim's memory and pass their legacy on to future generations.

To complete the installation of the commemorative sign dedicated to the "Pan" ships, we need your support. Together, we can give their story a place of honor on the shores of the land they so deeply longed to reach.

The Organization of Cyprus Ma’apilim has undertaken to raise ₪320,000 for the entire commemorative project. Of this amount, the "Pan" community’s fundraising target is ₪40,000.

Historical Profile: "Kibbutz Galuyot" and "Atzmaut"

Port and date of departure Burgas, Bulgaria – 27 December 1947
Number of Ma'apilim Approximately 15,500 – the largest single Aliyah Bet operation.
The voyage Severe overcrowding in the ships' holds, a high degree of self-discipline, and singing on the decks.
The encounter at sea Upon leaving the Dardanelles, the ships were surrounded by British destroyers. An agreement was reached to sail directly to Cyprus to avert a disaster involving mass casualties, and on 31 December 1947, British forces boarded the ships as they made their way towards the detention camps.
In the Cyprus detention camps The Ma'apilim from the "Pan" ships formed the largest group in the detention camps until their release. Many subsequently took part in the War of Independence and helped build the state.

Professor Snunith Shoham, Chair of the Organization of Cyprus Ma’apilim | The "Pan" Ma'apilim Heritage Team
